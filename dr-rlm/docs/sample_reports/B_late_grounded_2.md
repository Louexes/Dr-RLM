## Question
1.	What is the current level of EU dependency on key agricultural inputs?
o	Soybean meal?
o	Maize?
o	Ethanol?
o	Fertilizers?


## Root report (as scored; inline `<cite id=...>` tags shown verbatim)


The European Union currently exhibits a **high level of structural dependency on imports** for key agricultural inputs, particularly in livestock feed ingredients (soybean meal and maize) and energy production (ethanol). This reliance persists despite the EU's significant domestic agricultural output, driven by the energy-intensive nature of producing these specific feed proteins and the gap between domestic biofuel production and consumption mandates.

### Soybean Meal and Maize: A Critical Feed Deficit
The EU's production of animal feed proteins relies heavily on imported grain and oilseed meals, resulting in **late 2020s estimates of roughly 75% self-sufficiency for all sources of feed protein**【4c9ab711-40】. This gap is primarily due to the inability of domestic mustards and sunflowers to match the high volume of soy required for efficient animal production.

*   **Soybean Meal:** There is almost total dependency on the Americas for this protein source. Data indicates that **MERCOSUR supplies more than 85% of soybean meal imports** to the EU, meaning the vast majority of the protein powder used to feed poultry, pigs, and cattle arrives from South America rather than being produced domestically【4c9ab711-48】. 
*   **Maize:** Similarly, the EU struggles to produce enough maize for industrial and feed uses. **Mercosur supplies more than 60% of maize imports** during critical growing seasons, creating a fragility in the supply chain tied to weather and trade dynamics abroad【4c9ab711-48】. Consecutive years of data show a surging import-to-production ratio for feed crops, increasing from 21% in 2021 to 39% in 2022, indicating that nearly 40% of the feed grain base is now external【4c9ab711-44】.

### Fertilizers: Energy Security and Geopolitical Exposure
Dependency on fertilizers has become a core strategic vulnerability. Over **15 to 30 percent of total agricultural input costs** are attributed to fertilizers, making the sector highly sensitive to global market shifts【acbb445e-6】.

*   **Nitrates and Import Reliance:** The EU's reliance on nitrogen fertilizers (ammonia and urea) has skyrocketed, rising from 32% in 2021 to **45% in 2022**【acbb445e-25】. Crucially, this volume is overwhelmingly imported from Russia, whose share of the EU fertilizer import market grew from 17% in 2022 to approximately **30%** in subsequent years【acbb445e-2】.
*   **Economic Shock:** This dependence on external gas markets has driven prices significantly higher. Even as recently as late 2025, fertiliser costs for EU farmers remained **62 per cent above 2020 levels**, forcing emergency measures like the temporary suspension of customs duties on key nitrogen imports to alleviate farmer pressure【acbb445e-3】【acbb445e-1】.

### Ethanol: The Biofuel Import Gap
The EU faces a **structural deficit** in fuel ethanol, driven by renewable energy mandates (RED II/III) that exceed domestic production capacity.

*   **Consumption vs. Production:** In 2023, the EU consumed approximately **5.19 billion liters of fuel ethanol** annually【fafa8dc5-3】. However, domestic production fails to meet this demand, leading to a reliance on external supply.
*   **Import Share:** The ratio of imports to EU ethanol production increased dramatically, rising from 21% in 2021 to **39% in 2022**【fafa8dc5-53】. This means that nearly 40% of the ethanol fueling the European transport sector and supporting industry is imported.
*   **Primary Sources:** This deficit is primarily filled by imports from **Brazil** through the Mercosur trade agreement. Brazil provides the soy and sugarcane logistics critical for meeting these quotas, though specific volumes vary, the trend confirms that the EU's ethanol self-sufficiency remains structurally low and import-intensive【fafa8dc5-58】.

### Conclusion
In summary, the EU's dependency on imported agricultural inputs is structural and severe:
1.  **Feed Proteins:** Self-sufficiency is roughly **75%**, with **>85% of soybean meal** and **>60% of maize** imports coming from Mercosur.
2.  **Fertilizers:** Nitrogen import reliance hits **~45%** (up from ~32%), with **~30%** originating from Russia, pushing input costs **62% above pre-crisis levels**.
3.  **Biofuels:** The EU imports roughly **39%** of its ethanol production needs to meet a consumption baseline of **5.19 billion liters**, relying heavily on Brazilian sugar cane and grain exports.
